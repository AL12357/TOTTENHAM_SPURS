Tottenham Hotspur

Backstory and trophy list, from 1882 to the present

Backstory

Founding (1882 to 1900s). Spurs began in 1882 as a club formed by schoolboys, who named it after Harry Hotspur, a figure from English history linked to the Percy family and the Tottenham area. They turned professional in 1895 and moved to the White Hart Lane ground in 1899. In 1901 they won the FA Cup as a non-league side, which is still a unique achievement for the modern era. They joined the Football League in 1908.

Golden era (1950s to 60s). Spurs won the league in 1950-51 and again in 1960-61. The second title came with the FA Cup, making them the first club of the 20th century to win the league and cup double. In 1963 they became the first British club to win a European trophy, beating Atlético Madrid to take the Cup Winners' Cup.

Cup success (1970s to 90s). Tottenham won the inaugural UEFA Cup in 1972, followed by more League Cups and FA Cups through the 1980s and into 1991.

Lean years (1990s to 2000s). Spurs were mostly inconsistent, with only occasional cup wins such as the League Cups of 1999 and 2008.

Pochettino era (2014 to 2019). Under Mauricio Pochettino Spurs became regular title challengers and Champions League qualifiers. They reached the 2019 Champions League final, losing 2-0 to Liverpool in Madrid, and moved into the new Tottenham Hotspur Stadium that year.

Managerial turnover (2019 to 2025). José Mourinho, Antonio Conte and then Ange Postecoglou followed. Postecoglou ended the club's 17-year trophy drought by winning the Europa League. Spurs beat Manchester United 1-0 on 21 May 2025, with Brennan Johnson scoring the only goal. That was also the club's first European trophy in 41 years. Thomas Frank was then appointed as head coach.

Present. As far as available information goes, the 2024-25 Europa League remains the club's most recent trophy. One source names a different head coach, so the current manager could not be confirmed. Check the club's official site for the latest.
